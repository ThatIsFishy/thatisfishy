Every tank runs on a small invisible process. Understanding it early saves fish.

## The three steps

Fish waste and leftover food break down into **ammonia**, which is toxic even in small amounts. One group of bacteria turns ammonia into **nitrite**, which is also toxic. A second group turns nitrite into **nitrate**, which is far less harmful. You remove nitrate with water changes, and plants use it as food.

A new tank has almost none of these bacteria. They live on surfaces, mostly in the filter, and they need time and a food source (ammonia) to grow.

## What "cycling" means

Cycling is growing those bacteria *before* you add a full load of fish. Plan on four to six weeks. During that time:

1. Add a source of ammonia, either a few fish food flakes each day or pure ammonia made for this purpose.
2. Test ammonia and nitrite every two to three days.
3. Watch ammonia rise and fall, then nitrite rise and fall, then nitrate start to climb.

The tank is cycled when ammonia and nitrite both read zero and nitrate is present.

## Two tips for beginners

- Use a water conditioner (dechlorinator) with every tap water fill. Chlorine kills the bacteria you are trying to grow.
- Don’t clean the filter media under the tap. Rinse it in a bucket of old tank water.
