It sounds backwards, but a small tank is harder to keep than a medium one.

## Why bigger is easier

In a small volume of water, one mistake changes the chemistry quickly. A bit too much food, one dead snail, a missed water change, and the readings jump. A larger tank dilutes mistakes, so conditions change slowly and you have time to react.

## A comfortable starting point

Around 60 litres is a good place to begin. It fits on a sturdy table or cabinet, leaves room for a small group of fish and some plants, and is still manageable for water changes.

## Before you buy

- **Weight:** water weighs about one kilogram per litre. Check that your furniture can carry it.
- **Space for equipment:** you need room for a filter, a heater and a light, and access to the back for cables.
- **Water changes:** think about how you will get water in and out. A bucket and a hose are fine.

Pick the tank first, then choose fish that suit its size.
